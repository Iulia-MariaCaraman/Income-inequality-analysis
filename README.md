# Income Inequality and Social Equity Analysis

Academic research project developed for the Economics course at ASE Bucharest.

## Overview
This paper investigates income inequality and relative poverty dynamics in Romania between 2019 and 2023, comparing national figures with European Union benchmarks. 

The study combines theoretical economic concepts with statistical data analysis to evaluate how minimum wage adjustments, public policies, and inflation impact wealth distribution.

## Key Theoretical Concepts
* Lorenz Curve: Graphical representation showing cumulative population percentiles against cumulative income shares.
* Gini Coefficient: Numerical index measuring deviations from complete equality, ranging from 0 for total equality to 100 for maximum inequality.
* Efficiency vs Equity: Balancing overall market productivity with fair resource distribution and decent living standards.

## Empirical Findings and Data Analysis
* Gini Index Trends: Romania recorded a general decline from 34.8 in 2019 to around 30 in 2023, showing more balanced distribution due to wage and pension increases, though inequality remains higher than the EU average.
* Relative Poverty Rate: People at risk of poverty dropped steadily from 23.8% in 2019 to 21.2% in 2023, indicating positive effects from social transfers.
* European Comparison: Evaluated Romania's position relative to countries with very low inequality, such as Slovakia, and high inequality, such as Bulgaria.

## Main Recommendations
* Modernizing tax collection systems: Upgrading IT infrastructure within public administration to reduce tax evasion and fund public education and healthcare.
* Fairer wage taxation: Easing the fiscal burden on low-wage workers to directly support disposable household income.
* Investments in education: Providing equal access to professional and digital skills training to encourage long-term social mobility.
